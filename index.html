0F   The Intersection · Accounting, Finance & Law

Three disciplines.
One operating view.

The lensesAccounting tells me what the records prove. Finance asks which choice creates the best valuation(s). Law tests what the documents permit and what survives when the plan fails. And AI becomes a cross-examination layer, not an answer engine.

The way I run AI in the background is different from simple prompting. I prefer to run a problem through all three lenses, in layers. Where the answers reinforce one another, the strategy accelerates. Where they conflict, the contradiction is the work. And that’s where raw and practical wisdom comes into play.

01Accounting
CPA
Chartered Professional Accountant · CPA Canada
  • Financial reporting and assurance
  • Management accounting and controls
  • Tax, finance, and governance

First, the CPA layer established the base of business and measurable views into operations. It established the evidence: accurate reporting, defensible controls, auditability, tax discipline, and the financial facts every decision depends on.

What do the records prove?

Accounting anchors the other two disciplines in evidence. I use AI to help reconcile ERP, bank, contract, and operating data, trace anomalies, and test strategic or legal assumptions against what actually occurred. My CPA judgement determines the accounting treatment and whether the evidence is defensible.

One example

Reported EBITDA shows covenant compliance. AI-assisted normalization flags capitalized costs and unsupported adjustments. Finance recalculates headroom; the legal lens tests the lender’s exact EBITDA definition. The refinancing strategy changes before a lender challenges it.

02Finance
MBA
Strategy, Finance, and M&A · University of Calgary
  • Strategic management and M&A
  • Corporate finance and valuation
  • Decision modelling and capital structure

Second, my MBA focused on finance. This layer taught me how to use that accounting evidence to compare choices, allocate capital, model uncertainty, and connect operating strategy to transactions and value.

Which choice creates durable value?

Finance converts evidence into choices. I use AI to broaden and accelerate stress tests across price, mix, capital, liquidity, and regime shifts. My finance and operating experience sets the assumptions, challenges the preferred strategy, and decides which conclusions survive accounting quality and legal constraints.

One example

An acquisition looks accretive. Accounting exposes working-capital and earnings-quality gaps; legal reveals change-of-control and priority restrictions. The model is rebuilt around cash that can move and rights that can be enforced.

03Legal
LLM
International Financial & Commercial Law · King’s College London · in progress
  • Corporate finance and secured lending
  • M&A and transnational restructuring
  • Financial regulation, risk, and capital markets

Third, my LLM layer connected the same financial rules and economics to legal rights, terms, covenants, priority, enforcement, regulation, and the documents that make a structure hold under scrutiny. It also gave me a lens into the depths of the global financial system.

What is permitted, enforceable, and senior?

Law tests whether the economically attractive answer survives contracts, covenants, priority, regulation, and a downside case. I use AI to compare those rights with cash-flow and valuation scenarios, while my legal and finance training guides the interpretation, the contradictions to pursue, and the questions that still require counsel.

One example

A succession structure maximizes headline value but places debt, guarantees, and owner liquidity in conflict. Legal exposes the limits; finance redesigns the capital stack; accounting defines the cash it can safely service.

What the mark means

Three disciplines. Multiple models. One governed system.

The three nodes represent the disciplines behind any meaningful business advancement: accounting, finance, and law. The centre is, metaphorically, an AI layer that checks, connects and cross-examines them as a secondary measure.

Sure, AI is the next order of research and analysis. It’s already advanced within single dimensions, arguably beyond any one of these three nodes on their own. But when a query or problem spans multiple nodes, AI still needs a fair amount of practical knowledge in each discipline to be useful.

As a standalone research tool, AI, when used wisely, is like a search engine to the 100th power. It’s a great tool. But in the hands of a novice, it can produce little more than noise. In the hands of a skilled craftsperson, fluent across multiple disciplines, it can create something closer to art.

In many mandates, I use AI as a second check: comparing diverse inputs and outputs across multiple LLM engines, orchestrating the work through my own secure agentic intelligence platform, and testing each conclusion against source data, contracts, and conventional professional judgment. The aim isn’t novelty. It’s faster, more accurate research, sharper contradiction, and decisions that remain defensible.

Multi-model AI · Agentic workflows · Advanced modelling & analysis · Accounting and ERP integration · Data verification & audit-ready controls · UX/UI & product design

Admittedly, I’m a bit of a nerd. I always have been. Oddly enough, my long history in UX/UI design, spanning youth and adulthood, has supported hands-on work in systems and process design and has now forged a solid base in AI. Tangentially, I know enough about the technology to build with it. I also know enough about accounting, finance, law, and operations to know when an answer is incomplete, unsupported, or wrong.

Governance and auditability

Every AI-assisted output is treated as evidence to test, not gospel. Conclusions remain traceable to the GL, bank data, contracts, and working papers so an auditor, lender, buyer, or lawyer can reconstruct what was done and why.

Tooling without lock-in

The workflow is model- and client-agnostic. The point is better decisions and a defensible evidence trail, not selling a platform or tying the business to one engine.
